# LendingClub_loan

As part of my SHAP analysis to interpret my loan default prediction model, I generated a SHAP summary plot that shows the top 10 features impacting the model’s decisions. The most influential feature is installment, which refers to the borrower’s monthly EMI. I noticed that when the installment amount is high (shown in red on the right), the model predicts a higher chance of default. This makes sense because borrowers with larger EMIs may struggle to repay, especially if their income is not sufficient. Next, I observed that annual income plays a big role. Borrowers with low income (shown in red) tend to increase the risk of default, whereas those with high income (shown in blue) push the prediction towards non-default. This confirms that lower income may not support debt obligations well.

The third most important feature is dti (Debt-to-Income Ratio). A high DTI value, meaning the borrower already has a lot of debt compared to their income, pushes the prediction towards default. Similarly, a high interest rate on the loan also leads to a higher risk, since it increases the cost of borrowing and repayment stress. The loan amount itself is also critical—larger loans are more difficult to repay and hence more likely to default. I also included a binary feature, home_ownership_RENT, which shows whether the borrower is renting. While its impact is smaller, the model indicates that renters may be slightly more likely to default than those who own homes.

Other features like public bankruptcies, number of mortgage accounts (mort_acc), total credit accounts (total_acc), and revolving utilization (revol_util) also contribute. Specifically, past bankruptcies significantly raise the default risk. On the other hand, having more mortgage or credit accounts slightly reduces the risk, possibly because it shows credit experience. Lastly, a high revolving credit utilization—how much of the credit card limit is being used—signals financial stress and increases the default likelihood.

In summary, I can say that my model clearly captures financial pressure indicators—like high EMIs, low income, large debts, and high interest rates—as strong predictors of default. This helps me understand how individual features influence model output and allows me to explain the model’s behavior in simple business terms.
